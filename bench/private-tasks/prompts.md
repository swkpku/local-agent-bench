# Private-data tasks — prompts

10 tasks across 4 domains people specifically want to run **locally** — the data (your bank statement, your bloodwork, your clients' invoices, your team's salaries) is exactly what you'd never paste into a cloud API. Data is **synthetic but realistic** (generated by `generate.py`, fully seeded) and answers are **deterministic + exact-match gradable** — no LLM judge.

These are harder than the DABench set: messier schemas (`MM/DD/YYYY` dates, signed amounts, `?`/blank fields, free-text descriptions), multi-step reasoning, and domain rules baked into the constraints. Modeled on how real benchmarks frame work (transaction categorization, lab-range flagging, AR aging, people analytics).

**Harness note:** inspect with `df.head()`/`df.columns`/`df.shape` — don't read whole files into the prompt; executed pandas reads from disk. Grade `@key[value]` output against `ANSWER_KEY.md`.

All paths assume you start the agent in `/Users/bobscott/Documents/github/localAI`.

## Personal finance — `finance/data/transactions.csv`
One year of checking/credit activity. Columns: `date` (MM/DD/YYYY), `description` (merchant), `amount` (debit = negative, credit/income = positive).

### F1 — recurring commitments audit
```text
You are a personal-finance coding agent. Write and run Python (pandas) to answer the question about the CSV below. Inspect columns/dtypes with df.head() first — do not paste the whole file. Parse the date column as MM/DD/YYYY. Follow every constraint exactly, then output each answer line in the exact format, nothing else on those lines.

File: /Users/bobscott/Documents/github/localAI/bench/private-tasks/finance/data/transactions.csv
Question: Find my recurring fixed-amount commitments (subscriptions, memberships, fixed bills) and how much they cost per month.
Constraints: Consider only debits (negative amounts). A recurring commitment is a (description, amount) pair that appears 6 or more times across the year with an identical amount to the cent. Count the number of distinct such commitments, and sum one charge of each (the magnitude, as a positive number) to get the total monthly recurring cost. Round the total to two decimals.
Answer format: @recurring_count[n]
@monthly_recurring_total[amount]
where n is an integer and amount is a positive float with two decimals.
```

## Health — `health/data/labs.csv`, `health/data/sleep.csv`
`labs.csv`: dated lab panels with `test, value, unit, ref_low, ref_high`. `sleep.csv`: 90 days of `sleep_hours, resting_hr, steps`.

### H1 — out-of-range labs + trend

Question: How many of my lab results are outside their reference range, across how many distinct tests, and what is my latest LDL value and its trend over time?
Constraints: A result is out of range if value < ref_low OR value > ref_high. Count total out-of-range results (each test+date is one result) and the number of distinct tests that have at least one out-of-range result. For LDL, report the value at the most recent date, and the trend as the literal word 'increasing' or 'decreasing' comparing the earliest to the latest LDL value.
Answer format: @out_of_range_results[n]
@distinct_tests_out_of_range[n]
@latest_ldl[value]
